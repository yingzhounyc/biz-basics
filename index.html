Glossary of Key Concepts

Tap any term to expand its definition and see a real-world example.

💰 Revenue

Definition: The total money your business brings in from sales before any costs are subtracted.

Formula: Revenue = Price per Unit × Number of Units Sold

If you sell 200 cupcakes at $4 each, your revenue is $800. Revenue is NOT profit — you still need to pay your costs out of it.

📦 Unit of Sale

Definition: The single item or service you deliver to a customer in one transaction.

For a bakery, it might be one cupcake. For a tutor, it could be one hour-long session. For a subscription box, it's one box. Defining your unit of sale clearly is the first step to understanding your costs and pricing.

🏷 Price per Unit

Definition: The amount you charge a customer for one unit of sale.

If you charge $4 for a cupcake or $60 for a tutoring session, that's your price per unit. Setting this too low means you can't cover costs; too high and customers may not buy.

💲 Direct Cost

Definition: Costs that can be traced directly to producing a specific product or service. These include both materials and labor.

Direct Materials: Raw inputs that become part of the product — flour, sugar, and butter for a cupcake, or fabric for a t-shirt.

Direct Labor: Wages paid to workers whose time is spent making the product. A baker decorating cupcakes, a tutor delivering a lesson, or a carpenter building a table — their hours can be tied to specific units produced.

If you pay a baker $20/hr and she spends 3 minutes decorating each cupcake, the direct labor cost per cupcake is $1.00. Combined with $1.50 in ingredients (direct materials), your total direct cost per cupcake is $2.50.

📋 Indirect Cost

Definition: Costs that support your business overall but can't be tied to one specific product. These include both overhead expenses and certain types of labor.

Indirect Overhead: Rent, utilities, insurance, accounting software, marketing — expenses that keep the business running but aren't tied to any single unit.

Indirect Labor: Wages paid to employees who support the business but don't directly produce the product. A bakery manager supervising the team, a receptionist answering phones, or a janitor cleaning the shop — their work is essential but can't be traced to a specific cupcake.

Your bakery manager earns $4,000/month overseeing all operations. Unlike the baker decorating cupcakes (direct labor), the manager's salary is an indirect labor cost — it supports everything but isn't tied to any one product.

📈 Variable Cost

Definition: Costs that increase or decrease based on how many units you produce or sell.

Key idea: More sales = higher variable costs.

If each cupcake uses $1.50 in ingredients, making 100 cupcakes costs $150 in ingredients and making 200 costs $300. The cost varies with volume.

📌 Fixed Cost

Definition: Costs that stay the same regardless of how many units you sell.

Key idea: You pay these even if you sell nothing.

Rent of $2,000/month is due whether you sell 10 cupcakes or 10,000. Other examples: insurance, salaries, loan payments, software subscriptions.

💸 Profit

Definition: The money left over after you subtract all costs from your revenue. This is what you actually earn.

Formula: Profit = Revenue − Total Costs

If your cupcake shop earns $3,000 in revenue and your total costs (rent, ingredients, packaging, etc.) are $2,200, your profit is $800. If costs exceed revenue, you have a loss.

📊 Profit Margin

Definition: The percentage of each dollar of revenue that becomes profit. It tells you how efficiently your business converts sales into earnings.

Formula: Profit Margin = (Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100%

If you make $800 profit on $3,000 revenue, your profit margin is 26.7%. A higher margin means more of every sale goes into your pocket rather than covering costs.

⚖ Breakeven

Definition: The point where your total revenue exactly equals your total costs — no profit, no loss. Selling beyond this point means you start making profit.

Formula: Breakeven Units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit)

If your fixed costs are $2,000/month, you charge $4/cupcake, and each cupcake costs $1.50 to make, your breakeven is 2,000 ÷ 2.50 = 800 cupcakes. Sell more than 800 and you're profitable!
